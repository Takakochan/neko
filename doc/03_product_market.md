# Product & Market Research — Detailed Steps

Your goal: the challenge is "build a robotics startup in 2.5 days." A tech demo alone isn't a startup, so your job is to make a convincing case for **who pays, why, and how much**. Takako presents, so you build **the substance of the presentation (slides, numbers, Q&A prep, mockups)**.

---

## Friday 19:30–22:00: Brainstorm the market

### 1. List customer candidates broadly (~1 hour)

Consider at least these four:

| Candidate | Likely pain | Reason to pay |
|---|---|---|
| Owners living alone / dual-income households | Cat is alone and bored during the day; lack of exercise, obesity | Relieving guilt, health |
| Multi-cat households | Each cat likes different things; hard to play with them together | Per-cat tailoring |
| Cat cafés / shelters | Staff don't have time to play with every cat; cat stress | Covering staff shortages, adoption rates |
| Pet hotels / vet inpatient wards | Cat stress in an unfamiliar environment | Service differentiation |

### 2. Research existing solutions (~1 hour)

- Automatic teaser toys, laser toys, motorized balls, toys with monitoring cameras
- For each: price range, number of movement patterns, whether it adapts to the cat, complaints in reviews
- **Look especially for whether "the cat gets bored quickly" is a common complaint.** If so, that's the evidence for why this product should exist

### 3. Gather supporting numbers

- Number of pet cats in the Netherlands and the EU
- Data on obesity / lack of exercise in indoor cats (veterinary organizations or papers)
- Size of the target segment (e.g. number of cat cafés / shelters in the Netherlands)

Always note the source URLs so you can answer if judges ask.

**Friday goal: a list of candidates and competitors.**

---

## Saturday 08:30–12:00: Narrow it to one

### 4. Choose the target

Agree with Takako at the 12:00 checkpoint. Criteria:

- Can an "expensive" piece of hardware like a robot arm be justified?
- Who gets the most value from "different play for each cat"?
- Can we convince the judges with a 2.5-day demo?

(My suggestion is **B2B for cat cafés and shelters**: with many cats, per-cat optimization is most valuable, a higher unit price works for a facility, and there's a social story around adoption rates and cat welfare. Make the final call based on your research.)

### 5. User persona

Describe one specific target user (name, role, daily routine, frustrations). One slide.

### 6. Business model

For example:
- Hardware sale / lease + monthly software subscription (personality profile updates, play logs, health reports)
- For facilities: number of units × monthly fee
- Future: use play data to flag health changes early (e.g. dropping activity levels)

Put placeholder pricing and a rough revenue estimate (facilities × adoption rate × price) on one slide.

---

## Saturday 13:00–16:30: Mock up the automatic training pipeline

Show on screen the core product experience: "upload videos and you get a model and personality profile for your cat." It doesn't need to actually work.

### 7. Screen flow (4–5 screens)

1. **Upload**: "Upload 2–3 videos of your cat playing, 30 seconds to 2 minutes each"
2. **Processing**: progress for auto-labeling → training → analysis
3. **Results**: cat name, personality type (e.g. "Patient Hunter"), feature radar chart, recommended play style
4. **Play log**: how many times the cat responded to each mode, daily activity level
5. (For facilities) **All-cats dashboard**: a card for each cat

Use any tool (Figma, slides, HTML). Match the look of Takako's radar chart.

---

## Saturday 16:30–20:00: Demo video and slide outline

### 8. Filming

- Record the moment integration first works (from the side, with both the arm and the on-screen dashboard in frame)
- From the cat footage, pick clips for the opening of the pitch: "cat looking bored" and "cat happily pouncing"

### 9. Slide outline (matching the pitch structure in `02_vision_integration_pitch.md`)

1. Title (product name, one-liner)
2. Problem
3. (Demo)
4. How it works (one diagram: video → personality → play style → learning from reactions)
5. Automatic training pipeline (mock screens)
6. Target market and persona
7. Business model and market size
8. How we differ from competitors
9. Next steps / team

Also come up with about three product name ideas.

---

## Sunday 08:00–11:00: Finish the slides

- Double-check every number has a source
- Cut text. One message per slide
- Prepare one slide with the recording embedded, in case the live demo fails

## Sunday 11:00–14:00: Q&A prep and rehearsal

### 10. Expected questions (prepare answers too)

- Why a robot arm? Isn't a cheap motorized toy enough?
- What about the cat's safety? (claws catching on the arm, the arm hitting the cat)
- Can a few videos really reveal "personality"? How accurate is it?
- What if the cat gets bored in a few days?
- What's the price? Who buys it?
- What about privacy of the data (cat videos)?
- Who are the competitors? Why will you win?
- What will you do in the next 3 months?

### 11. Rehearsal

- Be the timekeeper
- Play a judge and fire questions
- During the real thing: back up Q&A and handle switching to the recording if the demo fails
